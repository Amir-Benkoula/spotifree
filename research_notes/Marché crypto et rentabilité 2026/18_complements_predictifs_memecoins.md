## 1. Prediction-market arbitrage: the arXiv 2508.03474 paper and 2025–2026 evidence

### Takeaway
The only rigorous quantification is the peer-reviewed paper "Unravelling the Probabilistic Forest: Arbitrage in Prediction Markets" (Saguillo, Ghafouri, Kiffer, Suarez-Tangil; AFT 2025): about **$40M of realised arbitrage profit extracted on Polymarket over one year (1 Apr 2024 to 1 Apr 2025, per a search summary)**, split across two arbitrage types. That is HIST. data from before the 2026 taker-fee regime. For 2026 I found **no audited or independent evidence that retail traders earn money from Polymarket/Kalshi arbitrage**; what exists is bot-vendor marketing, plus one on-chain study (Meyer, seen only via secondary write-ups) reporting that simple two-sided arbitrage has largely stopped paying after slippage, fees and market efficiency.

### Cited Findings
**The paper (primary)**
- Authors: Oriol Saguillo, Vahid Ghafouri, Lucianna Kiffer, Guillermo Suarez-Tangil (paper pages are hosted by IMDEA Networks; affiliations not otherwise checked). Published at AFT 2025 (LIPIcs, article 27) and on arXiv as 2508.03474 — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03474); [Dagstuhl LIPIcs](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.27); [IMDEA](http://www.imdea.org/unravelling-the-probabilistic-forest-arbitrage-in-prediction-markets/) [primary]
- Headline: "a realized estimate of 40 million USD of profit extracted across both types of arbitrage during our measurement period" (abstract wording as relayed by the search summary). The summary of the paper's page puts the measurement window at **1 April 2024 to 1 April 2025** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03474); [Dagstuhl](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.27) [primary; window as reported by search summary]
- Two arbitrage types: **market rebalancing arbitrage** (within one market/condition, e.g. outcome prices not summing to $1) and **combinatorial arbitrage** (across related markets whose logical dependencies are violated). Method: on-chain historical order-book data, a heuristic reduction (timeliness, topical similarity, combinatorial relationships) validated by expert input, to find both when opportunities existed and when users actually executed them — [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03474v1); [Dagstuhl](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.27) [primary]
- Mechanism stated by the authors: Polymarket requires a set of conditions to be exhaustive and mutually exclusive, so related prices should sum to $1; when dependent assets are mispriced, one can buy (or sell) a certain outcome for less (more) than $1, "guaranteeing profit" — same sources.
- **Not retrieved:** the split of the $40M between the two types, the number of arbitrageurs, and per-wallet concentration. Secondary sources give conflicting dataset sizes (about 86 million bets in one secondary source; 17,218 conditions in a Substack; more than 7,000 markets in a blog post). A second search found no source for "86 million bets". Treat all dataset sizes as unverified — [Substack (Navnoor Bawa)](https://navnoorbawa.substack.com/p/arbitrage-evolution-from-morgan-stanleys) [opinion]
- A Flashbots Collective research post, "Arbitrage in Prediction Markets: Strategies, Impact and Open Questions", exists; content not retrieved — [Flashbots Collective](https://collective.flashbots.net/t/arbitrage-in-prediction-markets-strategies-impact-and-open-questions/5198)

**2026 evidence on arbitrage profitability (weak: mostly vendor and SEO)**
- An on-chain study by Thorsten Meyer, cited across several sites, covers **95 million Polymarket transactions from April 2024 to December 2025** and found that **only 0.51% of wallets earned more than $1,000**. Reported summary: the once-reliable "cross-side" arbitrage (buying both sides of a binary contract for less than $1) "has largely ceased to be profitable due to slippage, fees and market efficiency". Seen only through secondary write-ups (1023 Jack, Simple Mondays, StrongMocha syndications) — [1023 Jack](https://1023jack.com/market/are-polymarket-trading-bots-actually-profitable-the-math-behind-2026-s-predictio/); [Simple Mondays](https://simplemondays.com/technology/are-polymarket-trading-bots-actually-profitable-the-math-behind-2026-s-predictio/) [secondary; original study not seen]
  - **Conflict with first-pass data:** Sergeenkov (April 2026, ~2.5M wallets) found about 2% of wallets above $1,000 (see `10_marches_predictifs.md` §3). The two "wallets above $1,000" shares (0.51% vs about 2%) differ by a factor of ~4; the methods and wallet filters are unknown, so neither number should be presented as *the* share without caveat.
- Other pages say Polymarket-Kalshi cross-platform arbitrage "remains viable but is highly competitive and difficult to execute profitably for retail traders" — [Launchpoly](https://launchpoly.com/blog/polymarket-kalshi-arbitrage-guide); [Predik](https://predik.io/en/blog/arbitraje-polymarket-kalshi-oportunidades-traders-cuantitativos-en) [SEO/vendor]
- A bot vendor (ClawArbs) says edges are "real but smaller than in 2024" — [ClawArbs](https://clawarbs.com/blog/kalshi-vs-polymarket-arbitrage/) [vendor]
- Fee illustration on Kalshi: in one example, net profit falls from $0.03 to a loss at the highest fee tier (unit not specified in the summary) — reported in the same group of pages (attribution among Launchpoly/ClawArbs/Predik not pinned down) [vendor/SEO]
- Unverified viral claim: "a single trader generated $5.3 million in profit over one month by deploying a high-frequency trading bot on Polymarket's sports markets" — [Predik blog](https://predik.io/en/blog/arbitraje-polymarket-kalshi-oportunidades-traders-cuantitativos-en) [vendor; the same article itself treats it as an outlier; not verified]

### Inferences
- The paper establishes that arbitrage on Polymarket is real and sizeable in dollars ($40M in a year) but small relative to activity: $40M against Polymarket's roughly $9B of 2024 volume (The Block figure in `10_marches_predictifs.md` §1) is well under 1% (my arithmetic; the windows do not coincide, so order of magnitude only). The rule being exploited is mechanical ("related prices should sum to $1"), so this is plausibly a bot game rather than a manual retail one (my inference; the paper summaries I saw do not say who the arbitrageurs were).
- Since early 2026 the economics got harder for takers: using the published crypto coefficient (0.07), a taker fee of about $1.75 per 100 shares at a 50¢ price is cited by one vendor page; that is 3.5% of the $50 notional (my arithmetic). For the crypto category, an arbitrage whose gross edge is smaller than the fees on its legs is wiped out unless executed as maker (other categories carry lower coefficients, 0.03 to 0.05, per the first pass). This is consistent with the Meyer summary but is *not* tested in any study I found.
- No 2026 independent study quantifies how much arbitrage profit remains after fees. State this as a gap, not as "arbitrage is dead".

### Gaps
- Breakdown of the $40M by arbitrage type, number of arbitrageurs, top-wallet concentration: not retrieved (paper body not readable; searches returned abstract-level summaries only).
- Meyer's original study (methodology, wallet filters, source of the 0.51% figure): not found.
- No audited track record of a 2026 Polymarket/Kalshi cross-platform arbitrage bot (net of fees, capital locked, resolution-rule mismatches between venues).
- Academic follow-ups on 2025–2026 data: none found.

## 2. Market making, rebates, "high-probability bonds" and short-term crypto bots (Polymarket, 2026)

### Takeaway
Market making is the strategy best aligned with Polymarket's 2026 fee design (no maker fees, rebates funded by taker fees, plus separate liquidity rewards), but **no independent, audited figure of market-maker profit exists in what I found**. The profit numbers circulating ($10–200 per $10K per day; $150–300 per market per day) come from affiliate-style guides and are not usable as benchmarks. "High-probability bonds" (buying 95–99¢ outcomes) are described by practitioners as a payoff of many small wins against rare large losses, with settlement-layer risk; **no rigorous win-rate dataset exists**. Short-term (5/15-minute) crypto-market bots are documented almost only by sellers of bots; one seller admits more than half of its net comes from fee rebates.

### Cited Findings
**Fees, rebates, liquidity rewards (2026)**
- Revenue model: taker fees began rolling out in **January 2026** and were broadened in February and March; no maker fees; part of taker fees funds maker rebates, described by one guide as paid daily in "**pUSD**" (name as given by that guide; I did not check what this token is). Guides state rebate shares of **25% in most categories, 20% in crypto, 15% in sports**, while another lists finance "up to 50%" as of April 2026, and a third mentions a July 2026 change. Geopolitical markets are fee-free and so generate no rebates — [StartPolymarket, rebates](https://startpolymarket.com/learn/polymarket-rebates-rewards/); [Ideausher](https://ideausher.com/blog/how-polymarket-makes-money/); [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/prediction-markets/how-does-polymarket-make-money/) [SEO; rates not verified against Polymarket docs]
  - Consistent with the first-pass table (25/20/15%) but **a "finance up to 50%" claim appears in one guide**: unverified.
- Liquidity rewards are a **separate stream** from rebates: rebates are paid on fills against resting orders; liquidity rewards score the quality of resting quotes and are paid daily — [Laika Labs](https://laikalabs.ai/prediction-markets/polymarket-liquidity-rewards); [Polymarkets.co.il](https://polymarkets.co.il/en/guide/liquidity-rewards/) [SEO]
- Reported earnings (all unverified, single-site claims): liquidity rewards "typically $10–200 per $10K deployed per day in active markets"; professional operators "$150–300 per market per day" but only with co-located infrastructure and low latency; a maker filling exactly at the midpoint is "net positive from rebates alone" (a best case that depends on fee levels and fill rates) — [Laika Labs](https://laikalabs.ai/prediction-markets/market-making-on-polymarket); [StartPolymarket market making](https://startpolymarket.com/strategies/market-making/) [SEO; not verified]
- **Polymarket revenue (fills a first-pass gap):** Sacra estimates Polymarket reached **$1B of annualised revenue in June 2026** (an estimate; Polymarket is private) — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/polymarket/) [research estimate; reported through search summary]
- Concentration of maker profits (first pass, kept for context): 0.55% of profitable maker wallets capture 50% of maker gains in political markets, Dec 2025 to Feb 2026 (Solidus) — see `10_marches_predictifs.md` §3.

**"High-probability bonds" (95–99¢)**
- Core arithmetic described by practitioners: at 95¢ one failure erases 19 wins; at 97¢ one loss offsets about 32 wins; expect roughly 5 failures per 100 bonds bought at 95¢ if the price is fair. The strategy only works if your probability estimate beats the market price — [StartPolymarket, bonding](https://startpolymarket.com/strategies/bonding/); [X thread by "probability god", Dec 2025](https://x.com/probabilitygod/status/1996498793026429058) [SEO/opinion]
- A critical essay argues the approach is "effectively an unhedged short on the integrity of a settlement layer that has had at least one disputed governance episode" — [Medium, "Polymarket Theta Harvesting: Why It Breaks at 99 Cents"](https://medium.com/@jacek.jurczynski/picking-up-nickels-at-ninety-nine-cents-d4308907db2c); another Substack, "So-Called 'Bonds' in Prediction Markets" — [Whirligig Bear](https://whirligigbear.substack.com/p/so-called-bonds-in-prediction-markets) [opinion; not measured studies]
- A trader who runs these trades says he is "under-confident in the 95–98 price range" but avoids the strategy because of "weird resolution drama, hard to price tail risk" (as paraphrased by the search summary; source among the pages above, not pinned down) [anecdote]
- Fee effect: one 2026 guide notes the fee formula peaks at 50¢ and falls toward both extremes, so a 95¢ taker trade costs far less than the identical market at 50¢ — [DataWallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/top-polymarket-trading-strategies) [SEO]
- **No rigorous public dataset measuring win rates or blow-up frequency of 95–99¢ buys was found.** The April 2026 on-chain figure (84.1% of 2.5M wallets in loss) covers all Polymarket trading, not bonds specifically.

**5- and 15-minute crypto markets and bots (2026)**
- The market for such bots is crowded with paid listings and self-hosted scripts; profit claims are secondhand: a "$68 to $1.5M account" and a Yahoo Finance report of a bot "turning $313 into $414,000 in a month" are cited by sellers, neither verified — [Polbots](https://polbots.com/); [PolyMTradeBot](https://polymtradebot.com/) [vendor]
- A vendor page states that on **7 August 2026** Polymarket stopped settling short-term crypto Up/Down markets on a single price snapshot; both the "price to beat" and the closing price became time-weighted averages computed from Chainlink data. If accurate it weakens last-second sniping. Not confirmed on Polymarket's documentation — [PolyMTradeBot blog](https://polymtradebot.com/blog/python-polymarket-bot-bitcoin-up-or-down) [vendor; unverified]
- Fee burden: crypto markets cost about **$1.75 per 100 shares at the midpoint**, which "can wipe out thin edges" (consistent with coefficient 0.07 × 100 × 0.5 × 0.5 = $1.75, i.e. fee = coefficient × shares × p × (1 − p); my arithmetic check). The figure comes from one of the vendor/blog pages returned by the search (not pinned down; candidates: [Medium/Illumination](https://medium.com/illumination/beyond-simple-arbitrage-4-polymarket-strategies-bots-actually-profit-from-in-2026-ddacc92c5b4f), [Polbots](https://polbots.com/)) [vendor/opinion; not checked against Polymarket's fee documentation]
- One bot seller says its entries roughly pay the taker fee through timing, and that **more than half of the net comes from Polymarket's fee rebates at a high volume tier**, meaning returns depend on rebate programs rather than direction or speed — [Kairos](https://kairos.trade/compare/polymarket-bot) (attribution among the vendor pages not fully pinned down) [vendor]
- Speed claims ("sub-100 ms bots capture most arbitrage", "average opportunity lasts 2.7 seconds") come from a generic blog with no data source — [Medium/Illumination](https://medium.com/illumination/beyond-simple-arbitrage-4-polymarket-strategies-bots-actually-profit-from-in-2026-ddacc92c5b4f) [unverified]

### Inferences
- The only mechanically documented edge is **structural**: makers pay nothing and receive part of the taker fee; takers pay. Whether net market-making profit is positive after adverse selection is not shown by any independent data I found; the Solidus concentration figure (0.55% of makers take 50%) suggests most makers earn little.
- "Bonds" are a **negative-skew** trade: the strategy earns the same as selling insurance (small steady wins, rare large loss). Because there is no published win-rate dataset, any quoted "annual yield" is a claim, not evidence. The oracle risk (see §4) makes the tail fatter than the market price implies.
- **Fee arithmetic for bonds (my calculation, assuming fee = coefficient × shares × p × (1 − p), which matches the vendor's $1.75 figure but is not checked against Polymarket's documentation):** at 95¢ a crypto-category taker fee (0.07) is about $0.33 per 100 shares, and a 0.04 category about $0.19, against a gross profit of $5 per 100 shares if the bond wins. So fees take roughly 4–7% of the gross gain, small next to the −$95 per 100 shares of a single failure. The decisive variable is the failure rate and resolution risk, not the fee.
- The 5/15-minute crypto-bot ecosystem is mainly an **infrastructure and rebate business** (the bot sellers, plus the platform's fee income at the highest coefficient, 0.07), not demonstrated retail income.

### Gaps
- Actual earnings of independent market makers: no sourced case (named wallet or published statement) found. A search on named professional makers (Jane Street, Susquehanna and similar) and on Kalshi's maker programmes was not run.
- Polymarket's official liquidity-reward formula and daily budget; the official rebate schedule after July 2026: not checked against Polymarket's documentation.
- Settlement-rule change of 7 Aug 2026 for 5/15-minute markets: single vendor source.
- Kalshi market-maker programmes and economics: not researched.

## 3. Insider-trading cases and the regulatory response (Nobel 2025/2026, Maduro, "Green Beret", CFTC/DOJ 2026)

### Takeaway
In 2026 insider trading on prediction markets moved from rumour to prosecution. On **23 April 2026** the US arrested Army Green Beret **Gannon Ken Van Dyke** for Polymarket bets on the Maduro raid he took part in (about **$33,000 staked, more than $404,000 won**); he pleaded not guilty on 28 April and the CFTC filed a parallel complaint, described as its first insider-trading case on event contracts. On **27 May 2026** DOJ charges against Google software engineer **Michele Spagnuolo** ("AlphaRaccoon") were made public: about **$1.2M** allegedly earned on Polymarket markets about Google's 2025 "Year in Search" rankings; NPR calls it the second known federal criminal insider-trading case on a prediction market. Kalshi, a regulated centralised venue, polices insiders itself (fines, suspensions, employer disclosure since June 2026). The Nobel Peace Prize 2025 bets (Machado) triggered a Norwegian Nobel Institute probe; a later report says it concluded a possible illegal access to the Institute's website. The 2026 prize (9 Oct 2026) went to Navi Pillay, who was not on Polymarket's list, and no leak has been confirmed. Several bills are pending in Congress; I found none enacted.

## 4. UMA oracle resolution disputes (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Polymarket's resolution layer (UMA's optimistic oracle plus token-holder Data Verification Mechanism, DVM) is now a documented source of tail risk. Reported 2026 data: **more than 1,150 disputed markets in the first five months of 2026** (already above all of 2025), a reported WSJ analysis finding **whale-dominated votes** (top-10 wallets cast more than half of the votes in most disputed markets) and **conflicts of interest** (about one dispute in five included a voter holding a position in the market), and a disputed "Strategy sells Bitcoin by 31 May 2026?" market that reportedly ended in a NO ruling and a lawsuit. The March 2025 Ukraine-minerals market remains the reference case for a whale vote. All figures reach me through secondary pages; I did not see the WSJ article or court documents.

## 8. KOL paid promotions and KOL wallet behaviour (Kolscan)

### Takeaway
The best-documented KOL economics come from **ZachXBT's leak of 1 Sept 2025 (HIST., but still the key evidence)**: a price sheet covering 200+ influencers with per-post rates from **$50 to $60,000** and Solana payment addresses; **more than 160** were confirmed to have accepted payments (The Block's headline says "over 100"), and **fewer than 5 of about 160 labelled their posts as ads**. For 2026 I found **no new study of promotion prices and none of Kolscan copy-trader returns**; the wallet-level evidence is limited to a model-based paper (about 70% precision, low recall), a vendor dataset (buys outnumber sells 1.5 to 1, most buys go to zero) and the Kolscan acquisition episode (more than $3M made by connected wallets).

### Cited Findings
**Paid promotion**
- **ZachXBT leak, 1 Sept 2025:** a price sheet with per-post rates and Solana payment addresses for **200+ crypto influencers**; **more than 160 confirmed to have accepted payments from $50 to $60,000 per post**; **fewer than five of roughly 160** properly labelled the posts as paid advertising. ### Inferences
- KOL pay is a **fixed fee or allocation from the issuer, independent of follower outcomes**. The leak documents what influencers were paid, not what followers earned or lost; the two are only linked by inference (promoted tokens are overwhelmingly short-lived, see `05_memecoins_launchpads.md` §3).
- **Disclosure was rare:** fewer than 5 of about 160 is under 3% (my arithmetic). A memecoin post by a KOL should be presumed possibly paid unless disclosed.
- The rate range of $50 to $60,000 spans a factor of about 1,200 (my arithmetic), i.e. a heavy-tailed market in which the top handful earn far more than the rest; no median or mean was reported in what I saw, so a typical KOL's income cannot be stated.
- Taken with the wallet-level evidence (low precision at identifying skilled KOLs; most buys end at zero), the data do not support copy-trading KOL wallets as an income method. The absence of a 2026 audited copy-trader study is itself a limit, not a proof either way.
