## 1. The ICO comeback: platforms, models, EU/France eligibility

### Takeaway
A wave of new platforms drove the late-2025 "ICO revival": Echo/Sonar (bought by Coinbase for about $375M on 21 Oct 2025), Coinbase's own monthly token-sale platform (debut: Monad, 17–22 Nov 2025), Buidlpad, Legion/Kraken Launch (marketed as MiCA-compliant) and MetaDAO. By Q2 2026, though, the public-sale market had fallen to a five-year low (CryptoRank: $58M raised across 37 sales). The "revival" is really a handful of large, heavily oversubscribed flagship deals on top of a shrinking long tail.

### Cited Findings
**Echo / Sonar → Coinbase**
- Coinbase acquired Echo, the on-chain fundraising platform founded by Cobie, for about $375M in cash and stock. The deal was announced on 21 Oct 2025. — [CoinDesk, 21 Oct 2025](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/10/21/coinbase-buys-crypto-fundraising-firm-echo-for-usd375m); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/345168/coinbase-acquires-echo-375-million); [Coinbase blog](https://www.coinbase.com/blog/coinbase-acquires-echo-unlocking-the-future-of-onchain-capital-formation)

The second is **Sonar**, a self-hosted tool that lets founders run their own public token sales. Echo reportedly helped projects raise more than $200M across about 300 deals since March 2024. Sonar's first high-profile use was the **Plasma (XPL)** sale; outlets report its size as either "$500M" or "$373M". — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/345168/coinbase-acquires-echo-375-million); [NFTgators](https://www.nftgators.com/coinbase-acquires-on-chain-fundraising-platform-echo-for-375-million/)

### Inferences
- The market has two tiers. A few flagship, exchange-affiliated sales (Monad, MegaETH, Plasma, Zama, Falcon) drew hundreds of millions of dollars in commitments. Meanwhile the overall count and volume of public sales collapsed in Q2 2026 to levels last seen in 2020–2021. The "ICO comeback" story describes late 2025 better than mid-2026.
- Allocation mechanisms differ, and this shapes who gets allocations:
  - First-come-first-served plus a reputation quota: Kraken/Legion, where 20% goes to Legion Score holders.
  - Request-and-allocate windows: Coinbase.
  - Auctions: MegaETH's ceiling-price auction on Sonar, Aztec's continuous clearing auction, Zama's sealed-bid Dutch auction.
  - Market-governed raises with refunds: MetaDAO.
  - Heavily oversubscribed pro-rata/allocation sales: Buidlpad.
- **EU/France (inference, to verify):**
  - Kraken Launch/Legion is the only platform in these findings explicitly marketed as MiCA-compliant, so it is the most plausible regulated route for French residents.
  - Coinbase Token Sales was framed around US retail access; EU/FR eligibility was **not verified**.
  - MetaDAO is no-KYC: technically reachable, but participants have no MiCA-style investor protections.
  - Buidlpad's Falcon sale drew participants from 141 countries (see §2), which suggests broad geographic access, but France-specific eligibility was not verified.

### Gaps
- No data gathered on: **CoinList** in 2025–26; **Bybit, OKX and Bitget launchpads**; **Hyperliquid-related launches** (HIP-1/HIP-3 tokens, ecosystem TGEs); **Binance Alpha**; Coinbase's sales after Monad (names, sizes, performance); Legion/Kraken Launch sales in 2026; Echo/Sonar rules on France/EU (Sonar sales are set per project, so eligibility varies).
- Coinbase Token Sales' fee percentage and allocation algorithm (for example whether it favours small buyers) were not confirmed in this session.
- The $1.4B H1 2026 versus ~$448M Q1+Q2 discrepancy needs to be checked against CryptoRank's own dashboard.

## 2. Notable 2025–2026 public sales: ROI for public buyers (sale price vs latest price, Sept–Oct 2026)

### Takeaway
Measured against the **public sale price**, the flagship sales of late 2025 and early 2026 show mixed results:
- **Clearly up:** FF (roughly +160% to +230%, snapshot date uncertain), XPL (about +70%, sale price unverified), ZAMA (about +57%), PUMP (roughly +40% to +60%, sale price unverified).
- **Roughly flat:** MON (about +2% to +4% on 11 Oct 2026).
- **Down sharply:** MEGA (about −55% to −60%) and AZTEC (about −47% to −62%).

Two facts weigh heavily on the real profitability for an individual:
- **Massive oversubscription meant tiny allocations.** At Falcon, about 96% of committed money was refunded, for an average allocation of roughly $21 per participant.
- **Buying at or after listing was much worse than the sale price.** FF fell about 84% from its listing-week high, and ZAMA traded about 40% below its auction price just after TGE.

### Inferences
- **Fill rates (computed from the figures above):**
  - Falcon: $4M ÷ $112.8M ≈ 3.5%. That works out to about **$21 average allocation** per participant ($4M ÷ ~190,000). Even at about +160%, the average profit is about $33 per participant, while each committed $1,000 was locked during the sale.
  - Zama: $44M ÷ $118.5M ≈ 37%.
  - Buidlpad 2025: ~$35M ÷ ~$420M ≈ 8%.
  - MetaDAO's Rip Cars, at 127x oversubscription: under 1%.
  - Headline ROIs therefore overstate what a typical participant earned in dollars. The real return on committed capital is the ROI multiplied by the fill rate, minus opportunity cost and fees.
- **Average ticket:** Monad ≈ $3,134 per participant ($269M ÷ 85,820). Large flagship sales without hard per-wallet caps let bigger tickets through.
- **Sale price vs listing price:** for several deals the public sale price was well below the early secondary price (FF ATH 15.7x the sale price; MEGA ~2.2x at its April 2026 peak; ZAMA ~2.1x in September 2026). Buyers who entered at or after listing generally did much worse, consistent with the §5 statistics.

- **Selection bias:** these are the most visible, best-funded 2025–26 sales. The long tail of smaller ICOs/IDOs performed worse (half of 2026 ICOs underwater by May; record-low Q2 volumes). Tracker "ATH ROI" and "peak ROI" figures, such as Buidlpad's ~10x, measure prices almost nobody fully captured.

## 5. Post-TGE performance statistics (share below listing price, low-float/high-FDV, unlocks, market makers)

### Takeaway
Several studies agree that **about 80–85% of 2025 TGEs traded below their TGE price** by late 2025 or early 2026. Memento Research counted **84.7% of 118 tokens**, with a **median −71% in FDV and −67% in market cap**. DWF Labs found more than 80% (memecoins excluded), with typical 50–70% drawdowns within 90 days. Galaxy put it at about 85%. In 2026 the evidence is thinner but no better: half of the first six 2026 ICOs were underwater by May 2026, and MetaDAO's curated launches were roughly break-even on median but negative capital-weighted. Large locked-supply overhangs (for example about 77% of ZAMA still locked) remain a structural headwind.

### Cited Findings
- **Memento Research (Ash):**
  - 118 TGEs tracked in 2025; **100 (84.7%) trading below their TGE valuation**; only 18 (~15%) above.
  - **Median drop −71% in FDV and −67% in market cap.** — [CryptoRank news](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/fdbee-2025-token-tges-below-price); [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/new-crypto-tokens-failing-in-2025-85-below-tge-prices/); [The Crypto Times, 21 Dec 2025](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2025/12/21/2025-token-launches-struggle-as-most-trade-below-tge-prices/)

- **Galaxy Research:** about **85% of 2025 tokens below their TGE price**, according to secondary coverage. — [Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/85-of-2025-crypto-tokens-below-tge-price/); [Airdrops.com](https://airdrops.com/news/85-of-2025-crypto-tokens-now-trade-below-tge-as-sell-off-deepens)

  - As of about May 2026, half of the six completed 2026 ICOs traded below their offering price. — [Crypto Economy](https://crypto-economy.com/cryptorank-reveals-just-six-icos-closed-in-2026-as-market-activity-dries-up/)

### Inferences
- The studies measure against the **TGE/listing price**, not the private or public sale price. ## 7. Who structurally profits in primary markets vs public participants

### Takeaway
The evidence collected points to **platforms** as the most reliable winners. They take fees on proceeds or purchases whatever the token does, and Coinbase paid about $375M for Echo/Sonar. Insiders and early rounds own most of the supply (public tranches of 2.5–15% of supply in the sales above, with large locked balances unlocking over time). Market makers sit on both sides, in some cases as backers and as research publishers. Public participants receive small, heavily scaled-back allocations. They do best when they get in at the public sale price and sell into early liquidity. Buying at listing has historically been a losing strategy (§5). Insiders do not always win either: the Aztec data suggests some late equity investors are underwater.

### Cited Findings
- **Platform economics:**
  - Coinbase Token Sales charges no listing fee but takes a **percentage fee on proceeds**. — [WEEX](https://www.weex.com/news/detail/coinbases-new-token-sale-platform-debuts-with-monad-revolutionizing-fair-access-in-crypto-for-us-investors-224294)
  - Kraken Launch charges a **0.5% purchase fee**. — [KuCoin flash](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/kraken-and-legion-launch-public-sale-for-yield-basis-yb)
  - Coinbase paid about $375M for Echo, which had facilitated more than $200M of raises. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/345168/coinbase-acquires-echo-375-million)
- **Small public tranches:** MegaETH public sale 5% of supply (500M tokens) at a $999M valuation ([ICO Drops](https://icodrops.com/megaeth/)); YB 2.5% of supply ([Phemex](https://phemex.com/news/article/kraken-and-legion-launch-yield-basis-token-sale-with-200m-valuation-22531)); Aztec 14.95% ([MEXC](https://www.mexc.com/news/168247)); Falcon a $4M raise against $112.8M of demand ([Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/press-release/falcon-finance-sets-community-sale-record-on-buidlpad-with-113m-ff-token-commitment/)).
- **Multiple pre-public rounds:** MegaETH's allocation lists VC Allocation, Team & Advisors, Foundation/Eco Reserve, **Echo Round**, **Fluffle Round**, KPI Staking, Mainnet Campaign and Public Sale (Sonar). — [Tokenomist](https://tokenomist.ai/megaeth)
- **Lock overhang:** about 77% of ZAMA supply was still locked (DropsTab). — [DropsTab](https://dropstab.com/coins/zama)
- **Public priced below last private round (counterexample):** Aztec's public auction started at $350M FDV, "~75% lower than the implied network valuation based on the latest equity financing". — [MEXC news](https://www.mexc.com/news/168247)
- **Market makers in the loop:** DWF Labs both published the ">80% below TGE" study ([CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/over-80percent-of-2025-token-launches-are-trading-below-listing-price-reports-dwf-labs)) and is described as a backer of Falcon Finance ([Blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/flashnews/falcon-finance-ff-token-sale-on-buidlpad-350m-450m-fdv-4m-raise-no-lockup-tge)).
- **Allocation privileges:** up to 20% of Kraken/Legion sales are reserved for "Legion Score" holders, and the rest is first-come-first-served. — [KuCoin flash](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/kraken-and-legion-launch-public-sale-for-yield-basis-yb). MegaETH buyers could get a 10% discount in exchange for a 1-year lock. — [ICO Drops](https://icodrops.com/megaeth/)
- **Historical context (undated):** issuers sell tokens at discounted prices to early buyers. — [ICOBench](https://icobench.com/stats/ico-statistics/)

### Inferences
- **Order of who is most likely to profit (inference from the above, not a measured ranking):**
  1. Platforms and exchanges, through fees, listing volume and user acquisition.
  2. Early/private rounds with low entry valuations, even after lockups, when the public price sits well above their cost.
  3. Market makers, through spreads and loan/option structures. Their terms were **not verified** here.
  4. Public-sale buyers who get allocations and sell into the first days of liquidity.
  5. Secondary buyers at listing, who were the main losers in 2025.
- For a retail participant, what matters is **dollars actually allocated × (exit price ÷ entry price − 1) − fees − opportunity cost of locked or refunded capital**. In Falcon-type oversubscribed sales the allocation term is tiny (about $21 average), so even multi-x ROIs yield little. Large allocations are available mainly in sales that were not oversubscribed, and those are disproportionately the weaker deals (inference; adverse selection).
- **Survivorship bias:** social media and launchpad marketing highlight peak or ATH ROIs (for example "average peak ROI ~10x", "+800% in hours"). The base rates (85% below TGE; half of 2026 ICOs underwater by May; MetaDAO capital-weighted −5.2%) are much less flattering.

### Gaps
- No data collected on **KOL rounds** (allocation size, discount, unlock terms), **market-maker loan deals** (terms, prevalence), or measured VC/insider returns on 2025–26 TGEs.
- No data on launchpad revenues (Coinbase's token-sale revenue, Buidlpad/Legion fee income).
- Aztec's last equity-round valuation (implied about $1.4B) is an inference from the "75% lower" statement and needs verification.
